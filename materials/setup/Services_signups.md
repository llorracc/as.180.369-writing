**This page is the canonical source for every account and subscription used in
this course.** What each tool is for, what you need, what it costs, and what
happens when a trial runs out.

Do the signups before Class 2 — several tools are demonstrated live and you
cannot follow along without an account.

## GitHub

**Free.** Needed from Class 1 — it is where all your work lives.

- Create an account at <https://github.com>, linked to your **JHU email**.
- Write a short description of yourself on your profile.
- Accept the invitation to the class repository (check your email, or
  <https://github.com/notifications>), then **fork** it. See
  `contrib/README.md` (not included in this copy) for what to do next.

**Apply for GitHub Education now, not when you need it** —
<https://education.github.com/discount_requests/student_application>.
Verification is not instant: it asks for proof of enrolment and takes days.

It costs nothing and upgrades your personal account to **GitHub Pro** while you
are a student:

| | free account | with GitHub Education |
|---|---|---|
| **Codespaces** — a development environment in the browser | 120 core-hours, 15 GB / month | **180 core-hours, 20 GB / month** |
| Actions on private repositories | 2,000 min / month | 3,000 min / month |
| Rulesets and protected branches on private repos | not available | available |

Two honest caveats, so nobody applies for the wrong reason: the Student
Developer Pack's partner offers **include no reference manager** — no Paperpile,
no Litmaps, no Zotero — so it will not help with the one cost below. And see
[Copilot](#github-copilot) before counting on that.

## Paperpile

**A 30-day free trial, then about $50 for the year.** This is the only thing
this course requires you to buy. You need it working for the homework set in
Class 2.

### Signing up — four steps, in order

Get the browser or the account wrong and you start over.

1. **Use Google Chrome.** Not Safari, not Firefox, not Edge. Paperpile is built
   around a Chrome extension. If Chrome is not on your laptop, install it
   first: <https://www.google.com/chrome/>
2. **Sign in with your personal Gmail account** at <https://paperpile.com> →
   *Sign in with Google*. Your library belongs to that account, so use one that
   will still be yours after you graduate. (This is also the Google identity we
   share course documents to — see [your Google account](#your-google-account).)
3. **Start the 30-day free trial.** No credit card is required.
4. **Install the Chrome extension** —
   <https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/paperpile/bomfdkbfpdhijjbeoicnfhjbdhncfhig>.
   People skip this, and without it the one-click "save this paper" button that
   the whole tool is organised around does not exist. Check it took: the
   Paperpile icon should be in Chrome's toolbar.

### What it costs, and which plan

**Buy Regular. You do not need Expert.**

| | **Regular** — what you need | Expert |
|---|---|---|
| Academic rate | $4.15/month → **$49.80/year** | $5.75/month → $69.00/year |
| Collect and organise references | **yes** | yes |
| Store and read your PDFs | **yes** | yes |
| Cite in all styles, and BibTeX | **yes** | yes |
| Full-text search across your PDFs | — | yes |
| Notes and PDF annotations | — | yes |
| Shared folders and shared libraries | — | yes |

The difference is **$19.20 a year**. Everything the course asks of you is in
Regular: your JEP issue is built in *your own* library, and the TA folds the
finished issue into the course library afterwards. **You are never asked to
share anything**, which is the only Expert feature this course touches.

Prices are the academic rate — 50% off, requires an academic email.

### Three things that surprise people

- **Billing is annual, and only annual.** Paperpile's own answer to "Can I pay
  monthly?" is *"No, all our plans are billed annually."* So it is a single
  charge of about $50 covering twelve months. You cannot pay only for the four
  months this course runs. Budget for it as one charge, not a monthly fee.
- **The trial is a full Expert trial** — *"The trial gives you access to all
  Paperpile features in the Expert plan."* So during your trial you will have
  annotations and sharing, and **those disappear when you subscribe to
  Regular.** Nothing is lost — *"Your data and configurations are never
  lost"* — but do not build a habit on a feature you are about to give up.
- **When the trial ends, Paperpile stops.** There is no free tier: *"Once your
  trial expires, you cannot access Paperpile anymore."* Your data stays and can
  always be exported, but the tool is unavailable until you subscribe. **A trial
  started in Class 1 expires around 30 September; one started in Class 2, around
  14 October** — mid-course, in the middle of the literature work. Put the date
  in your calendar.

**There is no JHU site licence.** We checked with the libraries; nothing covers
Paperpile centrally, so this cost is yours.

*Checked against <https://paperpile.com/pricing/>, <https://paperpile.com/h/try-paperpile-free/>
and <https://paperpile.com/h/downgrade-plan/> on 2026-09-16.*

## Litmaps

**Free tier, and you are not expected to pay.** Used from Class 3.

- Sign up at <https://www.litmaps.com> with your **@jhu.edu** email.
- **What free gives you:** **2 Litmaps**, **100 articles per map**, basic search
  with up to **20 inputs**. Enough for this course, but not generous — two maps
  is exactly two topics, so do not burn one on an experiment.
- Pro is $10/month, about $120/year at the education rate. **You are not
  expected to buy it.** If you hit the limit, tell us.
- **What a Litmap actually is**, before you are asked to make one:
  [`materials/pitch/litmaps.md`](../pitch/litmaps.md).

*Checked against <https://www.litmaps.com/pricing> on 2026-09-14.*

## ChatGPT and Codex

**Free.** This is the AI agent the course actually uses, from Class 2.

- Sign up at <https://openai.com>.
- **The Codex CLI is included in the free plan** — *"ChatGPT Work and Codex are
  included in your ChatGPT Free, Go, Plus, Pro, Business, Edu, or Enterprise
  plan."* Command-line access works on Free; only the cloud features need Plus.

This is why the course uses Codex rather than [Claude Code](#claude-code): it is
the capable agent every student can actually run without paying.

*Checked 2026-09-11.*

## Claude Code

**Not required for this course, and not free.** Listed here so you can decide
knowingly rather than discover it after paying.

- **Requires a paid plan** — *"Requires a Pro, Max, Team, Enterprise, or Console
  account. The free Claude.ai plan does not include Claude Code access."*
- Pro is **$17/month billed annually, $20/month billed monthly**, and there is
  **no advertised free trial** of Pro.
- The free Claude.ai chat interface is fine and costs nothing; it is only the
  *terminal agent* that is paywalled.

*Checked 2026-09-11.*

## HopGPT

**Free with your JHED login**, at <https://genai.jhu.edu>.

Gives you Claude, ChatGPT and Llama **as a chat interface**. Useful, and it
costs nothing. Its limitation is worth knowing: it cannot authorise a terminal,
so it **cannot drive Codex or Claude Code** — it is for conversation, not for
agents working in your repository.

*Checked 2026-09-11.*

## Your Google account

**Free, and it must be a Gmail — this is not a stylistic preference.**

Everything the course shares with you goes through Google: the Paperpile
library, and the Google Docs and Sheets. **Google sharing is addressed to a
Google account, and an @jh.edu address cannot receive it.** If we have the wrong
address for you, you are silently left out of shared material and nobody
notices.

Use the personal Gmail you sign in to Paperpile with, and make sure the TA has
it.

## JHU library proxy

**Free with your JHED login.** Set up in Class 3.

This is how full-text PDFs arrive in Paperpile: Settings → Proxy connection →
Johns Hopkins → sign in with JHED (<https://proxy1.library.jhu.edu>). It is the
step that fails silently if skipped — references save fine and PDFs simply never
appear.
